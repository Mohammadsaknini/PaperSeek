# Abstract

Classical keyword-based literature search methods are often limited by their deterministic nature, which makes them unable to effectively capture semantically relevant works. As a result, they frequently retrieve irrelevant results that happen to share matching keywords. In this thesis, we address these limitations by introducing *PaperSeek*, a semantic search framework designed to enhance large-scale literature discovery by using research questions, domain-relevant context, synthetic document generation, and active learning-based re-ranking. Starting from an OpenAlex snapshot of over 100 million works in English with abstracts, we benchmark nine embedding models for literature retrieval and select Stella as the most suitable model for this task. We curate an evaluation dataset comprising 30 systematic literature reviews, which we use as a benchmark for literature retrieval.~Our experiments show that using five targeted research questions, together with the title and abstract of a publication that answers those questions and synthetically generating additional context, improves recall by 8.1 percentage points over traditional keyword-based methods. When further enhanced with our active learning-based re-ranking approach, incorporating human feedback for literature screening, the overall recall improvement increases to 15 percentage points. This work contributes to literature retrieval by introducing a scalable semantic search framework that enhances recall through question-driven matching, contextual augmentation, and active learning-based re-ranking, particularly within the context of systematic literature review workflows.
